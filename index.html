Why “Horkos”?

In Greek mythology, Horkos is the personification of the oath — and, more precisely, of what happens to those who break one. Hesiod names him a son of Strife, born to punish anyone who swears falsely. He is not a god of grand cosmic justice. His concern is narrow and unglamorous: you said a thing, and now you say otherwise, and someone should be keeping count.

That is what this project does. It keeps count.

The problem

Millions of Jehovah's Witnesses receive a notification in the JW Library app telling them that a publication has been updated. What they are almost never told is what was changed, or why. A paragraph is rewritten, a requirement is softened, a date is quietly dropped, a teaching is adjusted — and the new text simply replaces the old one. Unless you happened to memorise the previous wording, the previous wording is gone.

An organisation that asks its members to stake their lives, families and futures on the accuracy of its teaching should be willing to show its working. Silently revising the record is not the same as correcting it. The revision may be perfectly innocent — most are — but that determination belongs to the reader, and the reader cannot make it without the evidence.

What this site does

Horkos checks every English-language publication on JW.org against Watchtower's own catalogue, on a schedule. When a publication is revised, it fetches the affected articles, compares them against the version it stored previously, and records exactly which sentences changed. The Change Monitor lists those revisions in reverse chronological order: the original wording, the replacement wording, and a note on whether anything doctrinal or procedural appears to have shifted.

The intent is documentary, not polemical. Formatting fixes and typo corrections are labelled as such. Where a change looks substantive, the altered text is quoted so you can judge it yourself, and every entry links to the current official page so the comparison can be verified against the source.
