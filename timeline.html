    Watchtower’s account

    The informal tract society organized in 1881 was legally incorporated in Pennsylvania in 1884, with Russell as president, giving the preaching and publishing work a stable legal footing independent of any one person's life.

    Historical assessment

  3. 1914 1914 taught as the end of “the Gentile Times” Watchtower history
    Watchtower’s account

    Based on a chronology beginning with Jerusalem's fall in 607 B.C.E. and a 2,520-year “seven times” period from Daniel 4, Bible Students taught 1914 would mark the end of “the appointed times of the nations” and Christ's installation as heavenly King - an event they hold the outbreak of world war and other calamity confirmed.

    “1914 — A Significant Year in Bible Prophecy” →

    Historical assessment

    Russell had originally taught 1914 would bring visible Armageddon and the overthrow of earthly governments; when this failed to occur, the teaching was reinterpreted after the fact as marking Christ's invisible enthronement. The underlying 1874/1914 chronology traces to Adventist writer Nelson H. Barbour, and mainstream Assyriology rejects the 607 B.C.E. date, placing Jerusalem's fall at 586/587 B.C.E.

  4. 1916–1917 Russell's death and the Rutherford succession crisis Watchtower history
    Watchtower’s account

    Russell died on October 31, 1916. Joseph F. Rutherford, the Society's legal counsel, was elected president in January 1917 and continued the preaching work, introducing the companion magazine The Golden Age.

    A Time of Testing (1914-1918) →

    Historical assessment

    Rutherford's election triggered an acrimonious dispute with four of the Society's seven directors, who accused him of overstepping his authority; Rutherford had them legally removed and replaced. The conflict, compounded by publication of The Finished Mystery, split the movement - an estimated one-seventh of Bible Students left by 1919, founding splinter groups (the Pastoral Bible Institute, the Laymen's Home Missionary Movement) that still exist.

  5. 1917–1919 The Finished Mystery and Rutherford's sedition conviction Watchtower history
    Watchtower’s account

    Watchtower literature frames the 1918 arrest and imprisonment of Rutherford and seven associates as religious persecution tied to their neutral, anti-war Bible preaching amid wartime hysteria; their convictions were reversed on appeal.

    A Time of Testing (1914-1918) →

    Historical assessment

    The Finished Mystery (1917) predicted God would destroy Christendom's churches “wholesale” in 1918 and all governments by 1920, a prediction that failed. Separately, Rutherford and seven directors were convicted under the Espionage Act in June 1918 for allegedly obstructing military recruitment through the book's content, sentenced to 20 years, served roughly nine months at Atlanta, and had the conviction reversed on appeal in 1919 for an unfair trial.

  6. 1925 “Millions Now Living Will Never Die” and the 1925 failure Watchtower history
    Watchtower’s account

    Rutherford's widely publicized 1918-1920 message held that the “generation” alive in 1914 would witness the resurrection of faithful Old Testament figures - Abraham, Isaac, Jacob - to earth and the start of paradise conditions, expected around 1925.

    “Millions Now Living Will Never Die” discourse →

    Historical assessment

    1925 passed with no resurrection or paradise; the failure caused significant defections and public ridicule. The Society had built a mansion (“Beth Sarim”) in San Diego in anticipation of the patriarchs' return, which Rutherford himself lived in until his death. It is one of the most-documented failed predictions in the movement's history.

  7. 1931 Adoption of the name “Jehovah's Witnesses” Watchtower history
    Watchtower’s account

    At a Columbus, Ohio convention on July 26, 1931, Rutherford presented a resolution, based on Isaiah 43:10-12, adopting “Jehovah's witnesses” as the group's distinguishing name, intended to unify identity and separate the group from other Bible Student offshoots.

    Their Modern Development and Growth →

    Historical assessment

    Historians view the name change as both a doctrinal statement and an organizational consolidation move by Rutherford, cementing his authority after the 1917 split by giving loyalists a distinct identity separate from independent Bible Student groups that had broken away.

  8. 1940–1943 Flag-salute refusal and the Supreme Court reversal Watchtower history
    Watchtower’s account

    Watchtower literature presents Witness children's refusal to salute national flags, viewed as a form of image worship inconsistent with Christian neutrality, as a principled stand that - after initial legal defeat - was vindicated when the U.S. Supreme Court reversed itself and affirmed Witnesses' free-exercise and free-speech rights.

    “Defending and Legally Establishing the Good News” →

    Historical assessment

    In Minersville School District v. Gobitis (1940) the Court upheld compulsory flag salutes 8-1; amid a wave of mob violence against Witnesses nationwide following that ruling, the Court reversed itself three years later in West Virginia State Board of Education v. Barnette (1943), a landmark First Amendment ruling still cited for the principle that government cannot compel speech or belief.

  9. 1942 Rutherford's death and Knorr's succession Watchtower history
    Watchtower’s account

    Rutherford died on January 8, 1942. Nathan H. Knorr was elected president days later and initiated intensive training programs - notably the Gilead missionary school in 1943 - that expanded the preaching work internationally.

    Historical assessment

    Knorr's tenure is described by historians as shifting the organization from Russell/Rutherford-style singular charismatic leadership toward corporate, systematized management, including standardized theocratic schools and the 1950 New World Translation project, largely the work of Frederick Franz.

  10. 1945 Blood transfusion policy adopted Watchtower history
    Watchtower’s account

    Watch Tower literature holds that Genesis 9:4, Leviticus 17 and Acts 15's command to “abstain from blood” apply to transfusion as well as ingestion, making blood transfusion of whole blood or its primary components a serious sin to be refused even in life-threatening situations, with some specifics left to individual conscience.

    Jehovah's Witnesses and the Question of Blood →

    Historical assessment

    Neither Russell nor Rutherford had addressed transfusion; the prohibition emerged in 1944-1945 Watchtower articles under Knorr as transfusion became common medical practice, and was enforced via disfellowshipping from 1961 onward. Bioethicists and physicians have documented resulting patient deaths and the policy's later partial softening, including permitting some blood fractions and cell salvage from the 2000s.

    Jehovah's Witnesses and blood transfusions — Wikipedia →

  11. c. 1950–2016 Higher education discouragement policy Watchtower history
    Watchtower’s account

    Watchtower literature has for decades counseled young Witnesses to weigh “additional education” carefully against spiritual goals and full-time ministry, citing concerns about worldly influence, debt, and time cost, while stating the decision ultimately rests with the individual or family.

    Make Wise Decisions Regarding Additional Education →

    Historical assessment

    Independent researchers and journalists document decades of much stronger discouragement of university education specifically. The 2008 Pew U.S. Religious Landscape Survey found Jehovah's Witnesses had the lowest rate of four-year college completion of any major U.S. religious group, a finding commentators have tied directly to this teaching.

  12. c. 1952 Disfellowshipping and shunning formalized Watchtower history
    Watchtower’s account

    Watchtower teaching frames shunning of unrepentant serious wrongdoers - including baptized family members - as a loving, scripturally based provision meant to protect congregation purity and motivate repentance, not as punishment. The term was changed from “disfellowshipping” to “removal from the congregation” in 2024.

    Why Disfellowshipping Is a Loving Provision →

    “Removal From the Congregation” (2024 terminology change) →

    Historical assessment

    The modern shunning practice, including shunning of family members within the same household, is generally traced to a 1952 Watchtower article; prior practice withdrew a wrongdoer from “special fellowship” without full social shunning. Sociologists studying high-control groups cite JW shunning, including of minor children by parents, as a significant source of documented psychological harm.

    Watchtower’s account

    From the mid-1960s, based on a calculation of 6,000 years of human existence since Adam's creation ending in the fall of 1975, Watchtower publications suggested the start of Christ's millennial reign could align with that date, later characterizing this as implication rather than firm prediction.

  15. 1976 The 1976 Governing Body reorganization Watchtower history
    Watchtower’s account

    Effective January 1, 1976, administrative authority previously concentrated in the Society president was redistributed to the Governing Body, operating through six committees, described in Watchtower history as one of the most significant organizational readjustments in the religion's modern history.

    Historical assessment

    Scholars characterize this as a deliberate move, with Knorr's declining health a factor, away from single-leader control toward a plural, committee-based leadership model - though critics note the Governing Body's membership and decision-making remain largely opaque and self-perpetuating.

    Watchtower’s account

  18. 1992–2001 UN NGO affiliation and its exposure Watchtower history
    Watchtower’s account

    Watchtower's official teaching holds the United Nations to be an illegitimate, doomed institution - the “scarlet-colored wild beast” of Revelation - which is precisely the theological position that made the organization's own 1992-2001 NGO affiliation controversial once it was revealed.

    “The United Nations — A Better Way?” (general doctrinal position; does not address the NGO affiliation itself) →

    Historical assessment

    The Watch Tower Society of New York held Associate NGO status with the UN Department of Public Information from February 1992 until October 2001, reportedly to access the UN library. The Guardian broke the story on October 8, 2001; the Society requested termination of the association within days. The episode is widely cited as a major hypocrisy scandal given decades of Watchtower condemnation of the UN.

  19. 1995 The “generation” teaching change of 1995 Watchtower history
    Watchtower’s account

    The November 1, 1995 Watchtower explained that “generation” at Matthew 24:34 does not refer to a literal, measurable lifespan tied to 1914, but rather to “the peoples of the earth” who see the sign but do not mend their ways - removing the previous link to a calculable end date.

    1914 — The Generation That Will Not Pass Away →

    Historical assessment

    This was at least the fourth revision of the “generation” teaching since the 1940s, having moved from a roughly 40-year period, to a full lifetime, to those merely “aware” of 1914 events; scholars view it as a response to the aging-out of anyone who could plausibly remember 1914 as an adult.

  20. 2000–2001 Corporate restructuring: Christian Congregation of Jehovah's Witnesses Watchtower history
    Watchtower’s account

    A January 2001 Watchtower/Kingdom Ministry announcement explained that the Governing Body had approved new corporate entities - Christian Congregation of Jehovah's Witnesses, Kingdom Support Services, and the Religious Order of Jehovah's Witnesses - to better handle the diversity and scope of the work, alongside the existing Pennsylvania and New York Watch Tower corporations.

    New Corporations Formed →

    Historical assessment

    Religion journalists reported this as a governance shakeup amid a broader membership growth slowdown - annual baptisms fell from roughly 50,000 in 1988 to 30,000 in 2000. President Milton Henschel and six directors resigned on October 7, 2000, replaced by figures outside the Governing Body, decoupling corporate officer roles from Governing Body membership - read by observers as both a liability-management and succession-planning move.

  21. 2002 The two-witness rule and child sexual abuse policy Watchtower history
    Watchtower’s account

    Watchtower policy requires two witnesses, or a confession, before congregational judicial action can be taken against an accused wrongdoer, per 2 Corinthians 13:1 and Deuteronomy 19:15. A 2002 Office of Public Information statement said elders should report abuse allegations to authorities where required by law, regardless of the two-witness standard used for internal discipline.

    Love and Justice in the Face of Wickedness →

    Historical assessment

    Independent investigations - courts, royal commissions, journalism - have found the two-witness rule, applied to internal discipline, effectively shields sole-perpetrator abusers from meaningful congregational consequence and has been used in ways that discourage victims from reporting to police. Numerous civil lawsuits and criminal cases globally have centered on this policy and on non-reporting by elders.

  22. 2010 The “overlapping generations” teaching of 2010 Watchtower history
    Watchtower’s account

    The April 15, 2010 Watchtower introduced the “overlapping generations” understanding: two overlapping groups of anointed Christians - those alive in 1914 and those alive later whose lives overlapped with the first group - together constitute “this generation,” allowing the teaching to extend well past 1914 without a fixed termination date.

    1914 — The Generation That Will Not Pass Away →

    Historical assessment

    Critics and former members note this represents a reversal of the 1995 change, which had explicitly abandoned tying “generation” to any specific overlapping-lifespan calculation, and effectively re-introduces an openly elastic, unfalsifiable timeframe.
